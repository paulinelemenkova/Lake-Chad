% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{Lake Chad situated in the semi-arid region of African Sahel plays a vital role in hydrogeological balance of regional ecosystems, presents an essential water source for millions of people living around its shorelines, and provides a habitat for wildlife species including rare and migrating waterbirds. However, the lake has shrunk significantly since the 1960s, and continued to reduce in size and extent during recent decades. Trends in drying and shrinking of wetlands in Lake Chad area are caused by diverse environmental factors and changed climate patterns. The desiccation of the lake is threatening both people's wellbeing and environmental sustainability. This study focused on identification of changes in the Chad Lake water surface areas, wetland extend and associated land cover types. The methods include the use of the GRASS GIS 'i.maxlik' and 'i.cluster' modules for remote sensing data classification using three multispectral Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS images. The maximum likelihood discriminant analysis classifier was applied for image processing and analysis. Three satellite images on 2013, 2017 and 2022 were processed to visualise changes in the land cover types which reflect variations in water balance, wetland area and extent around the Lake Chad over the recent decade. The results demonstrate that scripting tools of the GRASS GIS provide an efficient method of digital image processing for monitoring land cover changes in endorheic lakes of Central Africa. GRASS GIS methods shown by this study provide an opportunity to better integrate Earth observation data in environmental monitoring by allowing automation of the cartographic workflow through scripts.}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Many environmental phenomena which occur in landscapes, agricultural and ecological structures present heterogeneous properties changing in space and time at various scales \cite{Pengetal2020,Fahrig,WILSON2017218}. Various approaches of modelling, visualisation and mapping are developed with aim at describing the nature of these structures, analysing their origin and trends in the dynamics. Using Earth observation data is one of the key approaches in geographical sciences for analysis of environmental phenomena. The principal idea of using remote sensing data for environmental monitoring consists in satellite image analysis which uses the information on spectral reflectance of the objects and land cover types on the Earth's surface visible from space identified as various digital values of the pixels on the images \cite{WEISS2020111402,Xieetal2008,ABDI2021108098}. Such fundamental properties of the remote sensing data enables to identify various classes of land cover types using image processing techniques. Furthermore, comparison of several images on the same area for various time periods enables to visualise and analyse the dynamics in land cover types. This is essential for evaluating landscape changes and climate-environmental monitoring. Such approach is possible using Landsat Operational Land Imager Thermal Infrared Sensor (Landsat OLI/TIRS) images.

This paper addresses the problem of mapping land cover types changes in the Chad Lake, Central Africa (Figure \ref{fig01}) using Landsat OLI/TIRS images and Geographic Resources Analysis Support System (GRASS) Geographic Information System (GIS).

In the past, Lake Chad was among the largest lakes of Africa with the extent varying according to climate fluctuations \cite{Daveau}. However, during the last 60 years, the lake experienced dramatic drying by around 90\% of its surface area which shrunk from $25000 km^2$ in 1960s to about $1350 km^2$  \cite{Owonikoko,Rudincova,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824,Zhaoetal2022}. The reasons for such significant changes include both natural and anthropogenic factors. Natural triggers for lake shrinkage consist of the complex interrelations of the hydro-climatic processes, e.g., increased temperature and decreased precipitation \cite{BUMA2016403,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824}. 

The unprecedented hydrological drought in the mid 20th century in sub-Saharan Africa significantly affected the lake volume \cite{Nzango}. Further climate effects included irregular rainfall patterns, changes in river discharge and rise in annual temperatures \cite{Pattnayak}. All these processes resulted in a series of droughts and desertification of the surroundings landscapes \cite{NWILO2020104190}. This leaded to the depleted vegetation and almost half of vegetation cover being lost during three decades until 2010s in the Lake Chad Basin \cite{NWILO2020104190}. As a consequence, the water level in lake declined dramatically. Aside from natural factors, the drying of the Lake Chad was exacerbated by the anthropogenic activity such as dams construction, artificial irrigation, overuse of groundwater resources and excessive water consumption \cite{Yaoetal,fishes8020064}. The cumulative effects from all these processes resulted in the shrinkage of the Chad Lake \cite{RIEBE2021104486}.  

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Accurate cartographic visualization has been a challenge in environmental sciences. It is even more essential for African studies where data are often scarce or vary significantly by sources, resolution and spatial extent. The wide range of the geographic data poses a great challenge for mapping approaches due to their different functionality \cite{STEINIGER2013136}. Selecting a suitable GIS program is important for environmental mapping of Chad Lake, since the functionality of the software may affect data analysis and limit cartographic techniques. A comprehensive survey is available on the existing free GIS \cite{LEIDIG201549,CHEN2010253}, recent trends in the development of cartography \cite{WangWu} and new directions and perspectives in GIS \cite{Lietal2022}, while this section covers the most relevant GIS applications for environmental mapping.

The efficient solutions for satellite image processing are presented by Earth Resources Data Analysis System (ERDAS) Imagine with applications of environmental monitoring \cite{FALAKI2020363} with many existing cases due to the functionality and availability of diverse modules aimed at raster data processing. The Global Mapper GIS, developed by the United States Geological Survey (USGS), includes a variety of tools including the Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) handling, 3D rendering, watershed delineation to process geological and environmental data. Probably the most-well known and widely distributed commercial GIS is the ArcGIS which process well both vector and raster data \cite{AlDjazouli}, supports satellite images processing \cite{SESNIE20082145}, enables hydrological catchment and basin analysis \cite{Nkiaka}, terrain DEM modelling for geospatial data analysis \cite{Kay}, spatial data visualization \cite{GHIENNE200281,Buenzli,Gachet}, etc. 

More examples of the commercial GIS software include Geomedia GIS, developed by the Hexagon Geospatial for cadastral mapping, terrain analysis, and landscape modelling. MapInfo GIS provides tools for environmental analysis, mapping and cartographic visualization. These and others proprietary GIS illustrate the commercial software on the geospatial market. However, such programs are restricted to the license availability which limits their use to large business enterprises and commercial organisations. This becomes inefficient for many research cases, such as educational workspace, private-run business or mapping for personal research needs. In such cases, the use of the open source GIS becomes essential for effective geographic data processing \cite{Auda}. 

\subsection{Related Works}

The existing examples of mapping Lake Chad include the use of ArcGIS \cite{Nzango}, ILWIS GIS \cite{Onamuti} or QGIS \cite{PhamDuc} for environmental monitoring. Other studies present cases of satellite image processing for hydrological modelling \cite{rs14061511}, estimating shrinkage in the catchment area \cite{s18072082}, variations in the soil characteristics in Chad watershed \cite{rs10060924}, or fluctuations in the Sahelian climate related to Chad region \cite{su131910734}. The fieldwork-based studies in Chad region use the hydrological parameters to estimate the net water balance in lake by modelling methods \cite{MAHMOOD2019104043}. The parameters include the tributary inflow, precipitation, evaporation, infiltration, and additional data on soil moisture deficit, soil imperviousness, or infiltration rate \cite{MAHMOOD2019122}. Such approaches, among others, partially address the questions of climate-environmental monitoring by simultaneously exploring multiple hydrological parameters. 

However, the existing methods handle only the relatively small-scale and restricted processes limited to the target hydrological variables and probabilistic modelling \cite{Guganesharajah} without their extend becoming converted to the indicators of land cover changes and visualised changes in landscapes. Besides, they often require hydrological surveying, \emph{in-situ} sampling and data collection \cite{Robinson} aimed at converging and adjusting models. In contrast, more advanced methods of environmental monitoring include the use of remote sensing data \cite{CRETAUX20061098,LEBLANC201187} and satellite images \cite{rs11212534,Coe,info14040249}. Using satellite images enable to perform time series for analysis of lake extent and associated land cover changes. With this regards, the advanced software GRASS GIS with embedded advanced modules of satellite image analysis achieves attention in environmental mapping owing to its powerful technical functionality and cartographic support \cite{jmse11040871}. 

As briefly reviewed above, the available GIS programs have limitations and may be efficient for selected operations, while restricted for automatic and complex image analysis analysis and accurate classification. This is mainly because these programs are designed for special needs of geographical data analysis, but lack programming scripts which are required for automated data processing. Another concern relates to the origin of data and materials, which may be captured from multiple sources, have diverse projections and resolution \cite{Fassoni,Bastola,atmos14020232}. The combination of these tools in one research for dividing research tasks is cumbersome and not always convenient, which raises up the question of the console-based programs for image analysis. This is achieved in the GRASS GIS, owing to the embedded programming syntax, which ensures a flexibility in mapping process.

\section{Research Focus}

Lake Chad is an endorheic shallow lake of Central Africa originating from the large paleolake Chad, a relict of a giant Quaternary Megalake \cite{DRAKE2022107318}. Major sediment types include the outcrops of Quaternary (Pleistocene) and Tertiary (Neogene) formations, Figure \ref{fig02}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_02.jpg}
	\caption{Geologic units of Chad. Software: QGIS v. 3.22. Data source: USGS. Map source: author.
	\label{fig02}}
\end{figure}

Geologically, the Lake Chad presents an intracratonic sag basin with basement comprising a suite of crystalline rocks related to the Pan-African orogeny \cite{SCHUSTER2009603}. The geological units in Chad region are formed during late Precambrian (pCm) and are located within the Chad geological province, Figure \ref{fig03}. The surrounding provinces with connected watershed region include the Nubian Uplift on the north, Erdis Kufra basin in north-east Chad, which includes the prominent desert Mourdi Depression and is limited by the Paleozoic sandstones of the Ennedi Plateu (Figure \ref{fig01} and Figure \ref{fig03}). The Mourdi Depression presents a rocky valley and a grazing ground area located along a route from the Ennedi Plateau towards the northeast region of lake. 

Palaeoecological reconstructions enabled to identify relict Saharan river deltas, landforms and paleoshoreline of Megalake Chad \cite{LEBLANC2006230,LEBLANC200616}. Since the last glacial maximum, the transgressed lacustrine deposits in depression of the Lake Chad basin affected the aeolian geomorphology in catchment area through flooded accumulation of dunes in the Bodélé Depression in northern Chad and dune islands within Lake Chad \cite{BRISTOW201646}. The area of Chad Lake has varied significantly in size over the centuries over the past 50,000 years due to the impact of climatic fluctuations in Sahara since the Late Miocene \cite{DURAND198237,Servant1983,Pouyaud}, monsoon-driven hydrodynamics \cite{bouchette} and geological uplifts \cite{BURKE1976197}. As identified by the littoral sedimentary records, fine-grained deposits and diatoms, the Chad Megalake was one of the giant lakes in the Sahara existing in Quaternary during humid period \cite{DRAKE2022107318,w12113180}. 

Western and eastern segment of the Congo Precambrian Belt form a southern border of the Lake Chad region presented by the Late Proterozoic orogenic chain, Figure \ref{fig03}. These geological units form the blocks formed as a result of the crustal evolution of the area. The most of the Chad's granitoids were formed during the end of the Proterozoic \cite{KUSNIR1997549}. The Precambrian formations of Chad are dated by the Proterozoic Eon and include the series of sedimentary rock originating from volcanic material with significant mineral potential \cite{Wacrenier,wacrenier1958notice}. Nowadays, the lake has immense catchment area of the groundwaters from the neighbouring areas which exceeds the $\sim 500,000 km^2$ \cite{MAHAMATNOUR2022158152}. 

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_03.jpg}
	\caption{Geologic provinces in Chad. Software: QGIS v. 3.22. Data source: USGS. Map source: author.
	\label{fig03}}
\end{figure}

The associations of geologic setting with land cover types are linked through soil types. Thus, in the flooded plain of Lake Chad region, the main mineral type is is smectite clays associated to kaolinite and illite \cite{JEANPIERRE2019176} which supports grounwater hydrology and supplies water level in the lake during periods of droughts. In contrast to the rich groundwater supply, the surficial inflow of the southern segment of the Lake Chad is supplied up to 90\% from the Chari-Logone catchment \cite{earth2020020}. The Chari and Logone rivers originate in southern highlands of Cameroon and the Central African Republic \cite{Hutchinson}. The northern segment depends on the minor inflows from the Komadugu Yobe River originating in Nigeria, and the overflows from the southern basin \cite{Fougou}. Early Cretaceous clastic sediments are found in the West African Rift Subsystem of Chad \cite{GENIK1992169} which presents the extent of the East African Rift in the south-eastern region. A part of the Great Rift Valley developed during Pan African orogeny, the East African Rift System has geologic implications on tectonic and structural aspects of mineral resource distribution and groundwater level in Chad. 

The hydrology of Lake Chad plays a key role in the environmental sustainability of Africa through regulating energy and carbon cycles, participating global climate balance and as a key regional water sources \cite{Papa,Tranvik}. Moreover, Lake Chad plays a key social-economic role in the region through support of agriculture activities and fishery, and as a water source to over 30 M people in the surrounding countries: Chad, Cameroon, Niger, and Nigeria \cite{VAQUERO2021100935}. As a transboundary water source, Lake Chad maintenance the livelihoods for existence of local population such as fishing and farming  \cite{Sarch,resources6030041,Baroin}. Besides, it provides diverse eco-system goods and services, and natural resources such as firewood or livestock-rearing \cite{RIEBE2021104486}. This highlights the importance of measures on the environmental conservation of the lake and sustainable development of its resources. As a result, the agricultural activities of the Lake Chad region are concentrated around the lake and the two river systems which play major role in the agricultural economics of the region. 

Hence, the human-induced triggers contributing to the shrinking of the Lake Chad include the unsustainable agriculture with groundwaters used for irrigation \cite{Lemoalle}, the increasing demand for freshwater from the Chad Lake by local population \cite{Baroin} and the drilling of boreholes related to hydrogeologic activities on the development of large-scale irrigation system \cite{BIRKETT2000218}. The intensive modern irrigation methods and farming systems in agriculture practising include the use of fertilizers and farm machinery for maize and millet production that lead to land degradation \cite{NILSSON201615}. Further, the analysis of the vegetation patterns and surface water levels in Lake Chad demonstrated links of land cover types with rainfall fluctuations during wet and dry periods \cite{balme2006annees}, as well as the effects from seasonally contrasted climate \cite{GBETKOM2023159302}

\subsection{Methods}

Programming methods and scripts present a recent trend used in geospatial data processing that emerged as a powerful alternatives to the traditional GIS software \cite{8882177,technologies11020046,7248095}. Libraries are tuned as a collection of codes specially designed to optimise specific tasks such as computing vegetation indices, projecting the data, and then concatenate the code into longer scripts which perform the complete task of mapping. Such a partition of the workflow enables the machine to perform selected tasks in satellite image processing, and thus optimises computer memory and resources. In contrast to many conventional software, the GRASS GIS supports scripts for optimization of the data processing and cartographic workflow.

Among the diverse existing methods and software that can be used for satellite image processing, scripting-based approach of GRASS GIS stands apart due to its advantages and functionality. Scripts of GRASS GIS minimise the workflow through the repeated parts of the code used for processing the data and enable the automated detection and classification of the objects on the images \cite{,Lemenkova202301,10008194}. This significantly decreases the possibility of human-included errors and misclassification of the pixels and enables to process big datasets due to significant level of automation. Besides the environmental monitoring, geophysical and geological mapping may take advantage of scripting algorithms in mapping which enable to adjust and tune many parameters in the cartographic workflow for plotting maps \cite{9964623,Lemenkova202230}.

Motivated by the advantages of scripts and libraries in image processing and powerful technical functionality of the GRASS GIS software in remote sensing applications for processing open spatial data. The increase of the open spatial data arises a question of the effective tools for their processing. The most well-know tools for cartographic visualization and spatial analysis is GIS. While the range of GIS was limited by restricted functionality, modern technologies such as scripts GRASS GIS enable a wide range of techniques. Decades ago, principally new paradigms for cartographic data processing and mapping were proposed through scripting. 

The main idea of cartographic scripts is to automate and simplify mapping workflow, to increase repeatability of algorithms and precision of mapping. Such methods principally differ from the traditional GIS as scripts rely on a straightforward concept: every step in a mapping process is fully controlled by the lines of code, written according to the language syntax. In this way, hidden obstacles and bugs, often in traditional GIS, are avoided, and the complete procedure of mapping is improved. Compared to the existing GIS methods, scripting approach of the GRASS GIS can be considered as an advanced alternative method of cartographic visualization. 

\subsection{Image capture and import}

The images were captured from the original location in USGS EarthExplorer repository. The images were downloaded using the defined credentials with regard to cloudiness, extent, time of coverage of the Lake Chad, image IDs and access path to Landsat scenes. Every element in the data collection is treated as object parameters, among which the computational region was set by the 'g.region' module to match the scene of the Landsat images. The resolution of each image is moderate (30 m for multispectral bands of the Landsat OLI/TIRS sensors) with the file size of around 800-900 MB for the folder containing all necessary and auxiliary files for each scene. The image processing was performed on the MacBookAir Apple computer with chip Apple M1 which allowed to technically process large files with high amount of the required computer memory. 

Each image consisted of several tiles representing Landsat OLI/TIRS bands: multispectral visible bands, NIR, Panchromatic, Cirrus, two SWIR and two TIRS bands. Although the dimensions of the bands are identical, the resolution differs with 30 m for bands 1 to 7 and 9 (Cirrus), 15 m for panchromatic band, and 100 m for two TIRS bands. In this study, we only used the multi-spectral channels of the Landsat with resolution of 30 m. Therefore, one pixel on a image corresponds to the 30 meters on the Earth's surface in the original TIFF which determines the level of details of the vegetation patterns on the classification to this spatial dimension. The images were then read into the GRASS GIS environment using the 'r.import' modules. Each Landsat OLI/TIRS image contained 11 bands which were imported and processed individually using 'r.import' module. 

Selecting the multi-spectral bands from the original image was based on the 'r.import' module which collects raster data and reads them into a GRASS raster mapset environmentl using embedded GDAL library and reprojects the raster files into cartographic parameters defined for a mapset. For instance, Band 1 was imported using the code in Listing \ref{lst01}: 

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for importing the Landsat 8 OLI/TIRS image on Chad (here: a case of the bands of Landsat image on November 2013),style=mystyle,label={lst01}]
r.import input=/Users/polinalemenkova/grassdata/Chad/LC08_L2SP_185051_20131122_20200912_02_T1_SR_B1.TIF output=L8_2013_01 resample=bilinear extent=region resolution=region --overwrite
\end{lstlisting}

 (here for 2013), and repeated likewise for relevant Landsat bands for 2013, 2018 and 2022. Alternatively, raster files can be imported using the 'r.in.gdal' library of GDAL and the images were stored in the working folder using location and mapset of the current project using code in Listing \ref{lst02}:
 
 \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for importing Landsat images on Chad using GDAL,style=mystyle,label={lst02}]
 r.in.gdal input=/Users/polinalemenkova/grassdata/Chad/LC08_L2SP_185051_20131122_20200912_02_T1_SR_B7.TIF output=L8_2014_07 --overwrite
 \end{lstlisting}
 
Once the images were read in the GRASS GIS environment, several steps of the developed framework on image processing were implemented, as described below. The metadata of the images were checked using relevant modules of the GRASS GIS. In particular, the techniques of image processing in GRASS GIS include the auxiliary functions of 'g.list rast' and 'r.info' acting on imported raster bands and showing the identified parameters of images as attributes and a given set of identifiers of technical specifications of the multispectral images: resolution, minimal and maximal values, coordinate system. After image preprocessing, the 'i.group' module was used for grouping the bands before the classification of the Landsat images using code in Listing \ref{lst03}:

 \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for grouping the bands of the Landsat image using 'i.group',style=mystyle,label={lst03}]
i.group group=L8_2013 subgroup=res_30m input=L8_2013_01,L8_2013_02,L8_2013_03,L8_2013_04,L8_2013_05,L8_2013_06,L8_2013_07
 \end{lstlisting}

This approach partitions the image using the embedded algorithm of discriminating the pixels on the images that represent various land cover types in target area using their spectral reflectances. We evaluated three images covering the region of the Lake Chad on the three years: 2013, 2017 and 2022. 

%----------- subsection ----------->
\subsection{Image classification}

Two major approaches in processing satellite images include the unsupervised and supervised methods of classification. The main difference between these approaches reside in the way the images are processed as data and the discrimination of pixels on the image scene. While the first approach requires human-based supervision that might be prone to errors, the unsupervised classification is a machine-based method which is more objective and free of bias. In this study, we use the unsupervised classification method using maximal likelihood classifier. The 'i.group' module of the GRASS GIS was used for processing the structures of the spatial data and grouping them into classes using the k-means algorithm. The classification was performed using the maximal likelihood approach by the 'i.maxlik' GRASS GIS module, which classifies the cells on the images using their spectral reflectance. 

The processing workflow for each image included clustering of images on each scene extracted from the original raster file, detection of clusters with centroids, assignment of pixels to these clusters based on individual spectral reflectance values and indicating their representative values by creating signature file. Technically the procedure was performed using the  code in Listing \ref{lst04}:

 \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for clustering the Landsat 8 OLI/TIRS image by k-means using 'i.cluster' module,style=mystyle,label={lst04}]
i.cluster group=L8_2013 subgroup=res_30m signaturefile=cluster_L8_2013 classes=12 reportfile=rep_clust_L8_2013.txt --overwrite
 \end{lstlisting}
 
In such way, it detects lans cover classes for each image for a short-term sequence of images on 2013, 2018 and 2022. The land cover classes were identified dividing the image into repetitive  segments of vegetation patterns automatically using the maximum likelihood approach of the GRASS GIS. The 'i.maxlik' module of the GRASS GIS was used for automatic generation of land cover classes based on the minimal distance search algorithm. This algorithm assigns pixels to the classes based on the distance between pixel's value and centroid of the tested class. In this procedure, it uses the signature file generated automatically by the 'i.cluster' module with indicated number of target classes. In GRASS GIS, the image classification was performed using the code in Listing \ref{lst05}:

 \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for image classification of the Landsat 8 OLI/TIRS images by the maximum-likelihood discriminant analysis classifier using 'i.maxlik' module,style=mystyle,label={lst05}]
i.maxlik group=L8_2013 subgroup=res_30m signaturefile=cluster_L8_2013 output=L8_2013_cluster_classes reject=L8_2013_cluster_reject
\end{lstlisting}

Regular intervals of the land cover classes in the Lake Chad region are represented in Figures \ref{fig05}, \ref{fig06} and \ref{fig07} showing landscape patterns for 2013, 2018 and 2022, respectively. Identifying of vegetation patterns and wetlands around the flooded area of the lake was done for target years using the detected land cover classes interpreted by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) classification available for Lake Chad region. Annotating the land cover classes was performed using signature file by identifying the breaks of intervals between the target classes using the difference in the spectral reflectance values of the pixels (cells on the Landsat image). Mapping the land cover types was performed using the code in Listing \ref{lst06}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption=GRASS GIS code for mapping land cover classes of the Landsat  images,style=mystyle,label={lst06}]
d.mon wx0
g.region raster=L8_2013_cluster_classes -p
r.colors L8_2013_cluster_classes color=roygbiv -e
d.rast L8_2013_cluster_classes
d.legend raster=L8_2013_cluster_classes title="2013" title_fontsize=12 font="Helvetica" fontsize=10 bgcolor=white border_color=white
d.out.file output=Chad_2013 format=jpg --overwrite
\end{lstlisting}

Similar classes (e.g., three types of savannah) were grouped based on the original resolution of the images (30 m) which enabled to identify vegetation patches with size larger that this size. In this way, the classes were grouped and located closely to the patches of landscapes with similar spectral reflectance values in the original Landsat images. The algorithm performed automatic clustering and classification of each image in less than 2 seconds and processed raster files for the years 2013, 2018 and 2022 using the described methods.

\section{Results}

The annotating of the land cover classes around Lake Chad based on recognised image features aimed at accurate object recognition during classification of the satellite images which required preliminary examination of the images and analysis of major landscape types according to FAO classification. Hence, the following 17 classes were selected and identified, according to FAO: 1) gallery forest and riparian forests; 2) woodland; 3) swamp forest; 4) savannah (Sahelian short grass savannah and herbaceous savannah); 5) steppe; 6) agriculture and irrigated areas; 7) settlements and urban areas; 8) bare soil; 9) rocky land; 10) sandy areas; 11) water bodies; 12) wetlands and floodplain with aquatic plants. Image features and associate land cover classes were recognised in the images, identified and described as patches of land cover classes constituting the landscapes of the Lake Chad.

\section{Discussion}

Visualising the spatio-temporal changes in the borders of the Lake Chad is essential for environmental monitoring in Chad region. The basin of he Lake Chad is a subject to the challenges due to  anthropogenic impacts and variability in climate patterns. Therefore, mapping changes using satellite images on different dates enables to assess changes in the lacustrine landscapes of the Chad Lake. The anthropogenic effects may be diminished to the adaptive ecological monitoring measures, as well as sustainable agriculture and farming in Chad Lake region. Advanced approach to the visualization of the land cover types in Sahelian Africa is through cartographic processing of the remote sensing data \cite{rs12152437,Lemenkova202229,rs15030560}. Using remote sensing data in mapping workflow becomes possible due to the recent development in space borne technologies which enable to apply advanced methods for monitoring environmental changes using satellite images \cite{Bardinet1981,Bardinet1983,rs9101032,Lemenkova202227,LI2021126934}. 

We have extended the studies on the variations of the Lake Chad and land cover changes in its surroundings. To evaluate the dynamics in the landscapes of the Chad Lake, we presented the application of the GRASS GIS algorithms of libraries i.maxlik and others for processing the three satellite images Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS taken on three years: 2013/Nov/22, 2017/Nov/17 and 2022/Dec/09. The idea is to visualise the changes in the land cover types by comparison of the images collected on the same calendar month for different years which enables to assess the changes. The objective approach of the unsupervised machine-based classification of the images strengthens the discrimination of the image represented as triplets for the multispectral bands of the Landsat images through the maximum likelihood classification within the frame of remote sensing theory on image properties and variations in spectral reflectance. 

Apart from the detected general trends in drying visible on the compared satellite images, there is a regional difference notable between the southern and northern segments of the Chad Lake, where the northern pool is demonstrates a larger extent of variability and and increased vulnerability to droughts, which is also proved in previous studies \cite{LEMOALLE2012247}. The reasons for such spatial heterogeneity in the desiccation of Lake Chad reflect local sensitivity to climate effects triggered by the regional differences in the rainfall patterns and vegetation patterns in the landscapes of the Lake Chad. Thus, as visible on the classified images ion several years (Figures \ref{fig05}, \ref{fig06} and \ref{fig07}), the remaining area of the permanent open water areas in the southern segment of the Chad basin is surrounded by a more densely vegetated swamps and small pools which helps to maintain a lacustrine environment and prevent the southern segment from desiccation, which supports earlier works \cite{Lauwaet}.

The proposed framework benefits from the GRASS GIS functionality and coding approach, and exhibits advantages of the Landsat OLI/TIRS scenes that balance the accuracy of visualization and complexity of the land cover types and retain sufficient discrimination threshold for image classification. Practical results are presented as maps based on the classified images. Finally, we effectively optimise the workflow of image processing and plotting using the alternative methods of scripts which are implemented in this work both in GMT and GRASS GIS, and evaluate them for mapping, annotation, and classification tasks. The GRASS GIS method is particularly efficient in terms of mapping production costs, since the toolset is free and open for use. However, although the range of technical functionality of GRASS GIS is wide, the use of scripts in cartography has certain limitations. In particular, it requires a familiarity with the programming paradigm, which supports scripts through the embedded language. 

In fact, the availability of the open source advanced cartographic programs, such as GRASS GIS, scales with the learning approach required to master the methods. In addition, the import of datasets is often performed via the integration with other libraries and Python. This article has presented a GRASS GIS script-based method for satellite image processing and cartographic visualization of land cover changes in the region of Chad, central Africa. Unlike previous traditional GIS-based methods of mapping which operate in the spatial domain, we performed plotting through the GRASS GIS syntax using codes which ensured the comprehensive workflow for the cartographic specification of the maps. The GIS-based methods relying on graphical user interface (GUI) tend to use the pre-defined designed tools and graphical methods of representation, while the proposed GRASS GIS-based method outputs maps which is made using individual adjustments in each lines of the programming code using expressions and syntax of the GRASS GIS. We have also compared the maps pairwise for various years and considered the underlying reasons for land cover changes with regard to the climate and environmental setting of the Sahelian region of Chad in central Africa.

\section{Conclusions}

Current study contributed to the environmental monitoring of the Lake Chad which has a long tradition, going back many years of hydrological surveying. Existing reports enabled to detect the changes in the actual level of the water horizons in the lake \cite{Tilho,LHuillier,Cabot,Tubiana,01440}. Evaluation of the environmental changes in the Lake Chad region relied on the estimation of the climatic data series \cite{Riou} and hydrological models that assess the volume balance in the lake based on the field observations \cite{bader2011modele}. The presented study continued these works by presenting a scripting approach of the an advanced cartographic tool GRASS GIS to remote sensing data analysis aimed at detecting changes in the land cover types of the Chad Lake. 

As demonstrated in this study, the GRASS GIS, provides a logical language with a straightforward syntax for a console-based cartographic mapping using shell scripts for image processing. As a machine-based method method, it is a fast and effective technique, compared to the conventional mapping workflow. The application of GRASS GIS shown the advantages of scripting languages as part of the new directions in modern cartographic development and remote sensing data processing. Setting the parameters of the GRASS GIS codes enables to produce the highest possible quality of maps, including matching the projection and colour palette tables (CPT).

The algorithm approach of scripts by the GRASS GIS is largely based on using its syntax, which is very similar to the programming languages. The lines of codes perform operations controlling the appearance of the cartographic elements on the maps and form a script for presented maps plotted in JPG (or other bitmap raster formats) which can be converted to other formats. Apart from the traditional JPG, GRASS GIS recognises a variety of miscellaneous grid formats, and enables data capture via GDAL (r.in.gdal). Using GRASS GIS for satellite image processing and mapping lead to a higher quality of the produced maps, and lower time required for workflow due to the reduced time required for scripting, compared to the cumbersome and time-consuming data processing in traditional menu-based GIS. Thus, in contrast to existing software, GRASS GIS presents a free set of command-line tools and options for processing of geographic data in both raster and vector formats, and in particular, for satellite image processing.

The maps were plotted by the GRASS GIS scripts using the image analysis and classification of the Landsat scenes covering the region of Chad. The demonstrated methods included automated scripting approach that ensures high print quality and accuracy of maps, prepared using the high-resolution data. Rather than using handmade cartographic workflow as in similar GIS-based studies, the GRASS GIS scripting presents improved methodology that emphasises the importance of programming as a new trend in contemporary cartography and remote sensing.A useful extension of the presented study would be to automatically generate a series of maps covering particular periods of droughts in semi-arid climate of Africa for a complete environmental risk assessment. Adding more datasets, for example, climate data, could extend the variability of factors for risk assessment.

As demonstrated in this paper, processing Earth Observation data by GIS enables to better understand wetland areas in Sahelian Africa, support monitoring of the terrestrial water cycle and land cover changes in drying lakes \cite{Jones}. This study revealed the shrinkage of Chad Lake and visualised changes in associated land cover types caused by the cumulated effects from climate factors (droughts, increase in temperature and decrease in precipitation) and human-related activities.
