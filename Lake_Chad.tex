\section{Introduction}
Many environmental phenomena which occur in landscapes, geological and ecological structures present heterogeneous properties which vary in space and time at various scales. Various approaches of modelling, visualisation and mapping these structure are developed with aim at describing and analysing their origin, trends and nature. The principal approach of using the remote sensing data consists in classification of the satellite images using the spectral difference in the pixels on the images that represent diverse objects on the Earth's surface visible from space. Such fundamental properties of the remotely sensed data enables to classify the satellite images into various classes of land cover types. Comparison of several images captured on the same area for various time periods enables to visualise and analyse the dynamics in land cover types and landscape changes visible from the space borne data such as Landsat Operational Land Imager Thermal Infrared Sensor (Landsat OLI/TIRS) images.

In this paper, we address the problem of tracking changes in the landscapes of Chad Lake using Earth Observation data. In the past, Lake Chad was among the largest lakes of Africa with the extent varying between $10,000$ and $25,000 km^2$ \cite{Daveau}. During the last 60 years since the 1960s, the lake experienced the dramatic drying by around 90\% with a surface area being shrunk from $25000 km^2$ in 1960 to ca. $1350 km^2$, as reported in many publications \cite{Owonikoko,Rudincova,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824}. The reasons for such significant changes include both natural and anthropogenic factors. The first ones include the hydro-climatic factors and related processes such as increased temperatures and decreased precipitation and rainfall \cite{BUMA2016403,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824} which results in a series of droughts and desertification of the surroundings landscapes \cite{NWILO2020104190}. Thus, \cite{NWILO2020104190} reports the depleted vegetation and almost half of vegetation cover lost between 1984 and 2016 in the Lake Chad Basin. As a consequence, this leads to the recession of water level of Chad and changes in the shape of shorelines.

The human induced triggers contributing to the shrinking of the Lake Chad include the unsustainable agriculture with groundwaters used for irrigation \cite{Lemoalle}, the increasing demand for freshwater from the Chad Lake by local population \cite{Baroin} and the drilling of boreholes related to hydrogeologic activities and development of large-scale irrigation system \cite{BIRKETT2000218}. The intensive development of modern irrigation methods and systems of farming in agriculture practising include the use of fertilizers and farm machinery for maize and millet production that also contribute to the degradation of the landscapes \cite{NILSSON201615}.

Apart from general trends in drying, there is a regional difference between the southern and northern segments of the Chad Lake, where the northern pool is notable for a larger extent of variability and and increased vulnerability to droughts \cite{LEMOALLE2012247}. The local sensitivity to the desiccation of Lake Chad is triggered by the regional differences in the rainfall patterns and vegetation patterns in the landscapes of the Lake Chad. Thus, the remaining area of permanent open water in the southern segment of the Chad basin is surrounded by a more densely vegetated swamps and small pools which helps to maintain a lacustrine environment and prevent the southern segment from desiccation \cite{Lauwaet}.

Monitoring Lake Chad has a long tradition, going back many years for application of hydrological surveying which enables to detect the actual level of the water horizons in the lake \cite{Tilho,LHuillier,Cabot,Tubiana,01440}. Recent development in space borne technologies and approaches to the satellite image processing enabled to apply more advanced methods for monitoring environmental changes using remote sensing data \cite{Bardinet1981,Bardinet1983,rs9101032,Lemenkova202227,LI2021126934}.

\subsection{Study Area}

The geographical location of the Lake Chad is illustrated in Figure \ref{fig01}. The lake has immense catchment area which includes the groundwaters from the neighbouring countries Chad, Cameroon, Niger, and Nigeria. The surrounding relief nearby the Lake Chad basin is very flat and the lake itself is shallow, with a maximum depth around 7 m, the average depth 3,40 m for the lake in general, 2,45 m in its southern segment and 4,55 m in its north segment \cite{Pedro}. Approximately 80\% of the water of the Chad Lake inflows from the Chari and Logone river systems (Figure \ref{fig01}) which originate in the southern highlands in Cameroon and the Central African Republic \cite{Hutchinson}. The agricultural activities of the Lake Chad region are also concentrated around these two rivers.

\section{Discussion}
Visualising the spatio-temporal changes in the borders of the Lake Chad is essential for environmental monitoring in Chad region. The basin of he Lake Chad is a subject to the challenges due to  anthropogenic impacts and variability in climate patterns. Therefore, mapping changes using satellite images on different dates enables to assess changes in the lacustrine landscapes of the Chad Lake. The anthropogenic effects may be diminished to the adaptive ecological monitoring measures, as well as sustainable agriculture and farming in Chad Lake region.
