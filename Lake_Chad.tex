% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{Lake Chad situated in the semi-arid region of African Sahel plays a vital role in hydrogeological balance of regional ecosystems, presents an essential water source for millions of people living around its shorelines, and provides a habitat for wildlife species including rare and migrating waterbirds. However, the lake has shrunk significantly since the 1960s, and continued to reduce in size and extent during recent decades. Trends in drying and shrinking of wetlands in Lake Chad area are caused by diverse environmental factors and changed climate patterns. The desiccation of the lake is threatening both people's wellbeing and environmental sustainability. This study focused on identification of changes in the Chad Lake water surface areas, wetland extend and associated land cover types. The methods include the use of the GRASS GIS 'i.maxlik' and 'i.cluster' modules for remote sensing data classification using three multispectral Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS images. The maximum likelihood discriminant analysis classifier was applied for image processing and analysis. Three satellite images on 2013, 2017 and 2022 were processed to visualise changes in the land cover types which reflect variations in water balance, wetland area and extent around the Lake Chad over the recent decade. The results demonstrate that scripting tools of the GRASS GIS provide an efficient method of digital image processing for monitoring land cover changes in endorheic lakes of Central Africa. GRASS GIS methods shown by this study provide an opportunity to better integrate Earth observation data in environmental monitoring by allowing automation of the cartographic workflow through scripts.}

\section{Introduction}
Many environmental phenomena which occur in landscapes, geological and ecological structures present heterogeneous properties which vary in space and time at various scales. Various approaches of modelling, visualisation and mapping these structure are developed with aim at describing and analysing their origin, trends and nature. The principal approach of using the remote sensing data consists in classification of the satellite images using the spectral difference in the pixels on the images that represent diverse objects on the Earth's surface visible from space. Such fundamental properties of the remotely sensed data enables to classify the satellite images into various classes of land cover types. Comparison of several images captured on the same area for various time periods enables to visualise and analyse the dynamics in land cover types and landscape changes visible from the space borne Earth Observation data such as Landsat Operational Land Imager Thermal Infrared Sensor (Landsat OLI/TIRS) images.

% \textcolor{red}{\lipsum[1-1]

In this paper, we address the problem of tracking changes in the landscapes of the Chad Lake using Earth Observation data. The major problem is related to the dryness of the lake and changes in its present shape due to the climate changes (droughts and decrease in precipitation) and human-induced activities. Monitoring Lake Chad has a long tradition, going back many years for application of hydrological surveying which enables to detect the actual level of the water horizons in the lake \cite{Tilho,LHuillier,Cabot,Tubiana,01440}. These early approaches to evaluation of the environmental changes in the Lake Chad region relied on estimation of the climatic data series \cite{Riou} and hydrological models that estimate the volume balance in the lake based on the field observations \cite{bader2011modele}. 

The fieldwork based approaches that used the hydrological parameters enable to estimate the net water balance in lake using the hydrological modelling \cite{MAHMOOD2019104043}. The parameters include the data on tributary inflow, precipitation, evaporation and infiltration, and environmental setting such as soil moisture deficit, soil imperviousness, and infiltration rate \cite{MAHMOOD2019122}. Such approaches, among others, partially address the issue of climate-environmental monitoring by simultaneously exploring multiple hydrological parameters. However, they can handle only the relatively small-scale and restricted processes limited to the target hydrological variables and probabilistic modelling \cite{Guganesharajah} without their extend becoming converted to the indicators of land cover changes and visualised changes in landscapes. Besides, they often require careful hydrological parameters setting to converge and adjust to model and involve \emph{in-situ} sampling and data collection \cite{Robinson}.

Another approach is to perform the visualization of the land cover types through mapping and cartographic processing of the remote sensing data \cite{rs12152437,Lemenkova202229,rs15030560}. This is become possible due to the recent development in space borne technologies which enable to apply advanced methods for monitoring environmental changes using satellite images \cite{Bardinet1981,Bardinet1983,rs9101032,Lemenkova202227,LI2021126934}. Two major approaches in processing satellite images include the unsupervised and supervised methods of classification. The main difference between these approaches reside in the way the images are processed as data and the discrimination of pixels on the image scene. While the first approach requires human-based supervision that might be prone to errors, the unsupervised classification is a machine-based method which is more objective and free of bias.

Among the diverse existing methods and software that can be used for satellite image processing, scripting-based approach stands apart due to its advantages and functionality. Scripts minimise the workflow through the repeated parts of the code used for processing the data and enable the automated detection and classification of the objects on the images \cite{,Lemenkova202301,10008194}. This significantly decreases the possibility of human-included errors and misclassification of the pixels and enables to process big datasets due to significant level of automation. Besides the environmental monitoring, geophysical and geological mapping may take advantage of scripting algorithms in mapping which enable to adjust and tune many parameters in the cartographic workflow for plotting maps \cite{9964623,Lemenkova202230}.

Programming libraries is another recent trend used in geospatial data processing that emerged as a powerful alternatives to the traditional software \cite{8882177,technologies11020046,7248095}. Libraries are tuned as a collection of codes specially designed to optimise specific tasks such as computing vegetation indices, projecting the data, and then concatenate the code into longer scripts which perform the complete task of mapping. Such a partition of the workflow enables the machine to perform selected tasks in satellite image processing, and thus optimises computer memory and resources. In contrast to many conventional software, the GRASS GIS supports scripts for optimization of the data processing and cartographic workflow.

Motivated by the advantages of scripts and libraries in image processing and powerful technical functionality of the GRASS GIS software in remote sensing applications, we propose the use of its libraries for Landsat OLI/TISR satellite image classification. This approach partitions the image using embedded algorithm of discriminating the pixels on the images representing various land cover types in the Lake Chad area using their different spectral reflectances. We evaluated three images covering the same target region of the Lake Chad on the three years: 2013/Nov/22, 2017/Nov/17 and 2022/Dec/09. 

\subsection{Study Area}

The geographical location of the Lake Chad is illustrated in Figure \ref{fig01}. The lake has immense catchment area of the groundwaters from the neighbouring countries (Chad, Cameroon, Niger, and Nigeria) which exceeds the $\sim 500,000 km^2$ \cite{MAHAMATNOUR2022158152}. In contrast to the rich groundwater supply, the surficial inflow of the southern segment of the Lake Chad is supplied up to 80\% from only two rivers -- Chari and Logone (Figure \ref{fig01}) that originate in the southern highlands of Cameroon and the Central African Republic \cite{Hutchinson}. The norther segment depends on the minor inflows from the Komadugu Yobe River originating in Nigeria, and the overflows from the southern basin \cite{Fougou}.

The geological units in Chad region are formed by the processes of the Pan-African Orogeny during late Precambrian (pCm), Figure \ref{fig02}. These are the results of the crustal evolution of the area that terminated with most of the Chad granitoids being formed during the end of the Proterozoic \cite{KUSNIR1997549}. The Precambrian formations of Chad are dated by the Proterozoic Eon and include the series of sedimentary rock originating from volcanic material with significant mineral potential \cite{Wacrenier,wacrenier1958notice}. 

The agricultural activities of the Lake Chad region are also concentrated around these two river systems which play major role in the agricultural economics of the country. The surrounding geomorphology in the Chad Lake basin is not expressed which results in a very flat relief nearby the lake and its overall shallow bathymetry: the maximum depths are around 7 m, the average depths 3,40 m for the lake in general, 2,45 m in its southern segment and 4,55 m -- in its north segment \cite{Pedro}. The social-economic importance of the Lake Chad as a transboundary water resource is expressed in the maintenance of livelihoods for existence of local population such as fishing and farming  \cite{Sarch,resources6030041,Baroin}. This highlights the importance of measures on the environmental conservation of the lake and sustainable development of its resources.

In the past, Lake Chad was among the largest lakes of Africa with the extent varying between $10,000$ and $25,000 km^2$ \cite{Daveau}. However, during the last 60 years since the 1960s, the lake experienced the dramatic drying by around 90\% with a surface area being shrunk from $25000 km^2$ in 1960 to ca. $1350 km^2$, as reported in many publications \cite{Owonikoko,Rudincova,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824}. The reasons for such significant changes include both natural and anthropogenic factors. The first ones include the hydro-climatic factors and related processes, e.g., increased temperature, decreased precipitation and rainfall \cite{BUMA2016403,MAHAMATNOUR2021100824} which results in a series of droughts and desertification of the surroundings landscapes \cite{NWILO2020104190}. Thus, \cite{NWILO2020104190} reports the depleted vegetation and almost half of vegetation cover lost during three decades until 2010s in the Lake Chad Basin. As a consequence, this leads to the recession of water level in lake.

The human induced triggers contributing to the shrinking of the Lake Chad include the unsustainable agriculture with groundwaters used for irrigation \cite{Lemoalle}, the increasing demand for freshwater from the Chad Lake by local population \cite{Baroin} and the drilling of boreholes related to hydrogeologic activities on the development of large-scale irrigation system \cite{BIRKETT2000218}. The intensive modern irrigation methods and systems of farming in agriculture practising include the use of fertilizers and farm machinery for maize and millet production that also contribute to the degradation of the landscapes \cite{NILSSON201615}.

Apart from the general trends in drying, there is a regional difference between the southern and northern segments of the Chad Lake, where the northern pool is notable for a larger extent of variability and and increased vulnerability to droughts \cite{LEMOALLE2012247}. The local sensitivity to the desiccation of Lake Chad is triggered by the regional differences in the rainfall patterns and vegetation patterns in the landscapes of the Lake Chad. Thus, the remaining area of permanent open water in the southern segment of the Chad basin is surrounded by a more densely vegetated swamps and small pools which helps to maintain a lacustrine environment and prevent the southern segment from desiccation \cite{Lauwaet}.

An overall trend in dryness and desertification of sub-Saharan regions includes the expansion of the desert zone southwards, as well as disappearance of forests replaced by the savannah shrubs and grasslands. At the same time, there are reported fluctuations of the dry and wet phases in the Chad Lake region reconstructed for Quaternary period \cite{Rognon}. Those are supported by the modelled climatic oscillations that existed in central Africa as proved by the sedimentological and geomorphological analysis indicating the location of alluvial flood plains, terraces and lacustrine deltas in the past \cite{Servant1975}.

\section{Materials and Methods}
To perform image processing for Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS imagery, we apply the Geographic Resources Analysis Support System (GRASS) Geographic Information System (GIS) introduced by \cite{Neteler2004} using the major approach of 'i.maxlik' for unsupervised classification \cite{Neteler2008}. The embedded algorithms discriminating the pixels account for individual identities of the land cover types and objects represented on the satellite images using the information on their spectral reflectances. The map of the study are has been prepared using Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) following the existing techniques \cite{Lemenkova202228}.

\section{Discussion}
Visualising the spatio-temporal changes in the borders of the Lake Chad is essential for environmental monitoring in Chad region. The basin of he Lake Chad is a subject to the challenges due to  anthropogenic impacts and variability in climate patterns. Therefore, mapping changes using satellite images on different dates enables to assess changes in the lacustrine landscapes of the Chad Lake. The anthropogenic effects may be diminished to the adaptive ecological monitoring measures, as well as sustainable agriculture and farming in Chad Lake region.

\section{Conclusions}
We have extended the studies on the variations of the Lake Chad and land cover changes in its surroundings. To evaluate the dynamics in the landscapes of the Chad Lake, we presented the application of the GRASS GIS algorithms of libraries i.maxlik and others for processing the three satellite images Landsat 8-9 OLI/TIRS taken on three years: 2013/Nov/22, 2017/Nov/17 and 2022/Dec/09. The idea is to visualise the changes in the land cover types by comparison of the images collected on the same calendar month for different years which enables to assess the changes. The objective approach of the unsupervised machine-based classification of the images strengthens the discrimination of the image represented as triplets for the multispectral bands of the Landsat images through the maximum likelihood classification within the frame of remote sensing theory on image properties and variations in spectral reflectance. 

The proposed framework benefits from the GRASS GIS functionality and coding approach, and exhibits advantages of the Landsat OLI/TIRS scenes that balance the accuracy of visualization and complexity of the land cover types and retain sufficient discrimination threshold for image classification. Practical results are presented as maps based on the classified images. Finally, we effectively optimise the workflow of image processing and plotting using the alternative methods of scripts which are implemented in this work both in GMT and GRASS GIS, and evaluate them for mapping, annotation, and classification tasks.
